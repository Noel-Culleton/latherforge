*On screen:* WHY IS MY / SOAP CRACKING? · Five causes. Five fixes.

You unmould your soap and there's a crack running right across the top. Sometimes it's a hairline. Sometimes the whole bar has split open like dry earth.

Here's the good news first: in most cases, a cracked top is cosmetic. The soap underneath is usually fine.

In this video I'll show you the five most common causes of cracking, how to tell which one you've got, and exactly how to stop it next time. And I'll show you the one kind of cracking you shouldn't ignore.

## Ch 2: Cause 1: Soaping too hot
[crack_hot1, crack_thermo1, broll_pour1]

*On screen:* SOAPING TOO HOT · Hot oils + hot lye / = overheating

Cause one: you soaped too hot.

When your oils and your lye solution go in hot, the reaction runs fast and the loaf can overheat in the mould. Heat builds in the middle, the soap expands, and the top splits.

The fix is simple. Let your oils and lye cool before you combine them. Many soap makers work somewhere around room temperature to warm, and a thermometer means you can repeat the same temperature every batch. Write it down in your batch notes, so when a batch cracks you know exactly what changed.

## Ch 3: Cause 2: Too much insulation
[crack_towel1, crack_mould1, broll_mould1]

*On screen:* TOO MUCH INSULATION · Blankets trap heat / Less is more

Cause two: too much insulation.

Wrapping the mould in towels or blankets helps soap go through gel phase. But for a recipe that already runs warm, that extra heat has nowhere to go.

If your soap cracks regularly, try less insulation, or none at all. Leave the mould uncovered on a rack, somewhere away from heat. If you want to avoid gel completely, some soap makers put the mould in the fridge for the first day.

## Ch 4: Cause 3: Honey, milk and sugar
[crack_honey1, crack_milk1, broll_pour1]

*On screen:* SUGARS ADD HEAT · Honey · milk · juice / Beer · sugar

Cause three: ingredients that add heat.

Anything with sugar in it gives the reaction extra fuel. That means honey, milk, fruit juice, beer and of course sugar itself. These ingredients make lovely bars, but they're the number one reason milk and honey soaps crack.

When you use them, soap cooler, skip the insulation, and put the mould in the fridge or even the freezer for the first twenty-four hours. Your milk soaps will stay lighter in colour too.

## Ch 5: Cause 4: Accelerating fragrance
[crack_fo1, crack_trace1, broll_blend1]

*On screen:* FAST FRAGRANCE · Some scents / speed everything up

Cause four: a fragrance that accelerates.

Some fragrance oils and essential oils, especially florals and spice scents like clove and cinnamon, speed up trace and push up the temperature at the same time.

Test any new fragrance in a small batch first. Add it at light trace, stir it in by hand rather than stick blending, and be ready to pour quickly. And always use your supplier's skin-safe usage rate. Don't add extra to make the scent stronger.

## Ch 6: Cause 5: A recipe problem
[crack_crumble1, crack_scale1, broll_calc1]

*On screen:* CHECK THE RECIPE · Cracks + crumbly / = check your lye

Cause five is the one that matters most: a recipe problem.

If your soap is cracked and also crumbly, with liquid pockets or an oily layer that won't absorb, the cause may be too much lye. That usually comes from a measuring mistake, or a recipe that was never checked in a calculator.

If you suspect that, do not use the soap on skin. Re-run your recipe through a soap calculator, check your scale is weighing correctly, and if you can't work out what went wrong, it's safer to throw that batch out.

## Ch 7: Is cracked soap usable?
[crack_plane1, broll_cut1, broll_cure1]

*On screen:* IS IT STILL USABLE? · Cosmetic? Plane it. / Lye heavy? Bin it.

So, is your cracked soap still usable?

If the only problem is the crack, yes. Let it cure as normal, then plane or trim the top with a knife or a vegetable peeler. Many soap makers sell those bars as seconds or keep them for home.

If the soap is cracked and crumbly or has liquid pockets, treat it as lye heavy until you've checked your recipe. When in doubt, throw it out.
