*On screen:* DON'T BIN IT / REBATCH IT · Save a failed batch

Your batch seized. Or the swirl went wrong, you forgot the fragrance, or the bars came out ugly.

Before you throw it away, you can probably rebatch it. Rebatching means grating your soap, melting it down gently and pouring it into a mould again.

In this video I'll show you when rebatching works, when it doesn't, and exactly how to do it.

## Ch 2: When rebatching works
[rb_seized1, rb_ugly1, broll_cut1]

*On screen:* WHEN IT WORKS · Seized · ugly · no scent / Not for lye heavy

First, when should you rebatch?

It's ideal for a seized batch, a separated or ricey batch you've stick blended back together, a design you don't like, or soap where you forgot the fragrance.

What rebatching can't do on its own is fix a lye-heavy soap. Melting it again doesn't remove extra lye. If you suspect a lye-heavy batch, you would need to recalculate and add oil, and that's an advanced fix. If you're not sure how much extra lye is in it, it's safer to throw it out.

## Ch 3: Step 1: Grate the soap
[rb_grate1, rb_grate2, safety1]

*On screen:* GRATE IT · Small shreds / melt faster

Step one: grate the soap.

Use a cheese grater kept only for soap, or chop it into small pieces. The smaller the shreds, the faster and more evenly they melt.

If the soap is fresh, it can still be caustic, so wear gloves and goggles.

## Ch 4: Step 2: Add a little liquid
[rb_liquid1, rb_crockpot1, broll_pour1]

*On screen:* ADD A LITTLE LIQUID · Water or milk / Just a splash

Step two: add a little liquid.

Put the shreds in a slow cooker, or a heat-proof bowl over simmering water. Add a small splash of water or milk, just enough to moisten the soap, not drown it. Fresh soap needs less liquid. Older, drier soap needs a bit more. You can always add more, but you can't take it out.

## Ch 5: Step 3: Melt low and slow
[rb_melt1, rb_stir1, rb_crockpot1]

*On screen:* MELT LOW AND SLOW · Mashed potato / not liquid

Step three: melt it low and slow.

Cover it and heat it on low, stirring every so often. Don't expect it to turn liquid like melt and pour. Rebatched soap goes soft and gloopy, like mashed potato. That's what you want.

This can take an hour or more, depending on the amount. Keep the lid on so the moisture stays in.

## Ch 6: Step 4: Scent and mould
[rb_scent1, rb_mould1, broll_mould1]

*On screen:* SCENT AND MOULD · Fragrance at the end / Press it down

Step four: add fragrance and mould.

Take it off the heat, let it cool slightly, and stir in your fragrance or essential oil at your supplier's safe usage rate. Then spoon it into the mould and press it down firmly, tapping the mould to knock out air pockets.

The top will look rustic. That's part of the charm of rebatched soap.

## Ch 7: Step 5: Unmould and cure
[rb_cut1, broll_cure1, broll_cut1]

*On screen:* UNMOULD AND CURE · Next day · cut / Cure before use

Step five: unmould, cut and cure.

Leave it in the mould until it's firm, usually a day or two. Then unmould, cut and let it cure somewhere airy. Rebatched soap made from fresh soap should finish the normal cure time from the original batch date, and a little longer won't hurt.
