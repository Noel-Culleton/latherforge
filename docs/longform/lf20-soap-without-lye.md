*On screen:* SOAP WITHOUT LYE? / THE HONEST ANSWER · What the videos don't tell you

Can you make soap without lye? It's one of the most searched soap questions there is, and you'll find plenty of videos saying yes.

The honest answer is no. All real soap is made with lye.

But that's not the scary answer it sounds like. In this video I'll explain why, what no-lye soap kits really are, and why a properly made bar of soap contains no lye at all.

## Ch 2: What makes soap
[nl_oils1, nl_reaction1, broll_blend1]

*On screen:* WHAT MAKES SOAP · Oil + lye / = soap

Soap is what you get when fats and oils react with a strong alkali. For bar soap, that alkali is sodium hydroxide. For liquid soap, it's potassium hydroxide.

That reaction is called saponification. The oils and the lye change into something new: soap and glycerin. Without that reaction, you just have oil.

## Ch 3: Myth: baking soda
[nl_bakingsoda1, nl_oils1, broll_pour1]

*On screen:* BAKING SODA? · Too weak / Won't make soap

You'll see claims that you can use baking soda instead. You can't. Baking soda is far too weak an alkali to turn oils into soap. You'll get a greasy mess, not a bar.

The same goes for most "natural" lye substitutes. Traditional lye made from wood ash is still lye, just with an unpredictable strength, which makes it riskier, not safer.

## Ch 4: Myth: melt and pour
[nl_mp1, nl_mp2, broll_mould1]

*On screen:* MELT AND POUR · Lye was used / by the maker

So what about no-lye soap kits? Those use melt and pour base.

The manufacturer made that base with lye, and the reaction is finished before it ever reaches you. You're melting soap that already exists and pouring it into a new shape.

It's a brilliant way to start, especially with children, because you never handle raw lye yourself. But lye was still part of making it.

## Ch 5: Why good soap has no lye left
[nl_bar1, broll_calc1, broll_cure1]

*On screen:* NO LYE LEFT · Made right = / zero free lye

Here's the good news. In a properly made, cured bar, the lye is used up completely by the reaction. There's none left over.

Two things make sure of that. First, an accurate recipe, worked out in a soap calculator and weighed in grams. Second, a superfat, usually around five percent, which means a little more oil than the lye can react with. That leaves a safety margin of oil, never leftover lye.

## Ch 6: Lye safety basics
[safety1, nl_lye1, broll_pour1]

*On screen:* HANDLE IT SAFELY · Goggles · gloves / Lye into water

If you decide to make cold process soap, lye just needs respect.

Wear goggles and gloves. Always add the lye to the water, never water to lye. Mix it somewhere well ventilated, and keep children and pets out of the room. If you splash it on your skin, rinse with plenty of cool running water.

Do that, and it's no more dangerous than many things already in your kitchen.
