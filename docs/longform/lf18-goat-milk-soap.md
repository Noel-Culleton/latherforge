## Ch 1: Intro

*On screen:* GOAT MILK SOAP / WITHOUT SCORCHING · Three methods that work

Goat milk soap is one of the most searched soap recipes there is. And it's one of the easiest to get wrong.

Add lye straight into fresh milk and it can scorch, turn orange or brown, and smell strongly of ammonia. Then the soap overheats in the mould and cracks.

In this video I'll show you three ways to make goat milk soap that avoid all of that, so you get a creamy, pale bar every time.

## Ch 2: Why milk scorches
[gm_scorch1, gm_lye1, broll_pour1]

*On screen:* WHY MILK SCORCHES · Lye gets hot / Milk sugars burn

First, why does it happen?

When lye dissolves, it gets very hot. Milk contains natural sugars and proteins, and that heat can scorch them. That's where the orange colour and the ammonia smell come from.

A slight tan colour and a mild smell are normal with milk soap, and the smell fades as the soap cures. What you want to avoid is heavy scorching. The answer to all three methods is the same idea: keep the milk cold.

## Ch 3: Method 1: Frozen milk
[gm_cubes1, gm_icebath1, gm_lye1]

*On screen:* FROZEN MILK · Freeze it into cubes / Add lye slowly

Method one: frozen milk.

Freeze your goat milk in ice cube trays the day before. Weigh the frozen milk exactly as your recipe says. Use it in place of the water, at the same weight.

Put the jug of frozen milk in a bowl of ice water. Then add the lye very slowly, a little at a time, stirring as you go. Take your time. It can take several minutes. The milk should stay a pale yellow, not orange.

Wear your goggles and gloves throughout. Lye in milk is just as caustic as lye in water.

## Ch 4: Method 2: The split method
[gm_split1, gm_pour1, broll_blend1]

*On screen:* SPLIT METHOD · Lye in half the water / Milk at trace

Method two: the split method.

Here you dissolve your lye in only part of your liquid, as plain water, and let it cool. Many soap makers use about half. The rest of your liquid is goat milk, which you add to the oils, or blend in once you reach light trace.

Because the milk never touches concentrated lye, it's much less likely to scorch. Just check in your soap calculator that your total liquid still adds up to the amount the recipe needs.

## Ch 5: Method 3: Powdered goat milk
[gm_powder1, gm_oils1, broll_blend1]

*On screen:* POWDERED MILK · Easiest for beginners / Mix into oils

Method three: powdered goat milk.

This is the easiest way to start. You make your lye solution with water as normal. Then you mix the goat milk powder into a little of your liquid oil, or into a small amount of the water you've set aside, and blend it in at light trace.

There's no frozen milk and much less risk of scorching. It's a great first goat milk soap.

## Ch 6: Stop overheating and cracks
[gm_fridge1, crack_milk1, broll_mould1]

*On screen:* KEEP IT COOL · No blankets / Fridge for 24 hours

Whichever method you use, milk soap runs hot in the mould, because of those natural sugars.

So soap at a cooler temperature, don't insulate it, and put the mould in the fridge or freezer for the first day. That keeps the bar pale and stops cracks and overheating.

Then unmould, cut, and cure for at least four to six weeks, like any cold process soap.
