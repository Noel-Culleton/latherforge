*On screen:* WHY IS MY SOAP SWEATING? · It's not ruined.

You check on your curing soap and the bars are covered in tiny beads of liquid, like they've been sweating. Some soap makers call it glycerin dew.

It looks alarming, but in almost every case your soap is fine.

In this video I'll explain why it happens, how to stop it, how to store your bars so it doesn't come back, and the one situation where you should take a closer look.

## Ch 2: Why soap sweats
[sweat_macro1, sweat_humid1, broll_cure1]

*On screen:* IT'S THE GLYCERIN · Glycerin pulls water / from the air

When oils and lye react to make soap, they also make glycerin. In commercial soap a lot of that glycerin is often removed. In handmade soap it stays in the bar, and that's part of why handmade soap feels so nice.

But glycerin is a humectant. It pulls moisture out of the air. So when the air is damp, water collects on the surface of the bar as little droplets. That's the sweat.

## Ch 3: When it happens
[sweat_window1, sweat_kitchen1, broll_cure1]

*On screen:* WHEN IT HAPPENS · Damp weather · steamy rooms / Cold to warm

You'll see it most in humid weather, in steamy rooms like kitchens and bathrooms, and when bars move from somewhere cold to somewhere warm, like condensation on a cold drink.

High glycerin bars sweat more, so it's common with melt and pour bases too. Wrapping soap in plastic while it's still curing can trap moisture and make it worse.

## Ch 4: How to stop it
[sweat_wipe1, sweat_fan1, sweat_rack1]

*On screen:* HOW TO STOP IT · Wipe · move · air flow / Dehumidifier

The fix is about moving air and lower humidity.

Gently pat the bars dry with a paper towel. Move them to a cooler, drier room, away from the kitchen and bathroom. Space them out on a rack so air can get all the way around, and turn them every few days.

A small fan or a dehumidifier in the curing room makes a big difference, especially in an Irish or British winter.

## Ch 5: Storing bars so it doesn't come back
[sweat_box1, sweat_wrap1, broll_cure1]

*On screen:* STORE IT RIGHT · Breathable boxes / Wrap only when cured

Once your bars are cured, store them somewhere cool and dry, in cardboard boxes or paper rather than sealed plastic.

If you sell your soap, wrap or label it only once it's fully cured and dry. If you use shrink wrap, make sure the bars are dry first, and keep finished stock out of damp sheds and garages.

## Ch 6: Sweat or something else?
[sweat_compare1, broll_calc1, broll_safety1]

*On screen:* SWEAT OR SOMETHING ELSE? · Beads on cured soap = sweat / Oily pockets = check recipe

One check. Normal sweat appears on bars that otherwise look and feel fine, and it comes and goes with the weather.

If instead your fresh soap has pockets of clear or oily liquid inside it, or liquid weeping out of a crumbly, cracked bar, that's not sweat. It can mean the recipe or the measuring went wrong.

Wear gloves, don't use that soap on skin, and re-check your recipe in a soap calculator. When in doubt, throw it out.
