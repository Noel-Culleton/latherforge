## Buying soap supplies in Australia

Most soap-making guides are written for the US, where lye is called "lye". In Australia it's usually sold as **caustic soda**, and the big US suppliers either won't send it here or charge heavily to do it. Buying locally is simpler and cheaper.

## Caustic soda (sodium hydroxide)

Australian safety data sheets list sodium hydroxide as a **Class 8 dangerous good** for road and rail transport, and several list soap making as a recommended use. That's why some sellers restrict how they ship it, or ask you to pick it up.

Where makers buy it:
- **Soap-making suppliers.** The safest choice, because the product is sold for soap.
- **Hardware stores and supermarkets.** Australian forum users report using hardware-store caustic soda (for example, Diggers from Bunnings) without problems, and one reported a supermarket brand labelled 98% sodium hydroxide. These are personal reports, not checked specs.

**What to check on any caustic soda:**
- The label or safety data sheet says **sodium hydroxide, 98–99%+**, with nothing added.
- **Never drain cleaner blends.** If the label lists other ingredients, don't use it for soap.
- A sealed container. Caustic soda pulls moisture from the air, and damp beads weigh wrong.

## Oils, butters, fragrance and moulds

- **Aussie Soap Supplies** describes itself as Australia's largest provider of soap bases and silicone moulds, and also sells fragrance, essential oils, colours, clays and packaging. It says it sells at wholesale prices to home crafters and small businesses. Source: aussiesoapsupplies.com.au/about-us
- Forum users also mention specialist fragrance and essential oil suppliers. Those posts are several years old, so check the business is still trading before you order.

Olive, canola and coconut oil from the supermarket work for basic recipes. Check the label for pure oil, not a blend. Shea butter, cocoa butter and castor oil are usually cheaper from a soap supplier.

**Tip:** freight is the hidden cost in Australia. Combine heavy oils into one order rather than several small ones.

## Summer soaping

Hot weather speeds up trace and makes overheating and cracking more likely. Soap at cooler room temperatures, and keep curing bars out of direct sun and humidity.
