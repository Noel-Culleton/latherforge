## Is handmade soap a cosmetic in Canada?

Yes. In Canada, soap sold for cleansing is treated as a cosmetic. That's different from the US, where "true soap" is mostly outside FDA cosmetic rules.

Health Canada says the cosmetic rules apply to handmade products too, including ones sold at craft sales or by home-based businesses. Size doesn't matter: a market stall counts.

## The Cosmetic Notification Form (CNF)

Before or soon after you start selling, you tell Health Canada about each product.

- **Deadline:** within **10 days of your first sale** in Canada. You can file before you sell and enter a predicted first-sale date.
- **Who files:** the manufacturer or importer. If you make and sell your own soap, that's you.
- **What it asks for:** brand and product name, the form of the product (for example, a bar), what it's for, the ingredients and how much of each.
- **Cost:** there is no fee.
- **Changed a recipe?** If you change the formula, the product name or your contact details, file a revised notification within 10 days of the change.
- **Late?** If you don't notify within the 10 days, the product can't be sold after that window until it's notified.

Notification is not an approval. Health Canada doesn't test or approve your soap. It's a record that the product exists and what's in it.

**Tip:** each scent or recipe is usually a separate product. Keep a list of your recipes and their notification dates in one place, so a recipe change doesn't slip past the 10-day rule.

## Labels: what goes on a soap label in Canada

- **Ingredients in INCI names**, in descending order of amount. For soap, INCI names are usually the saponified oils, such as Sodium Olivate or Sodium Cocoate. Check Health Canada's labelling guide for how to list them.
- **English and French.** For products sold in Canada, the required label information must be in both languages. The INCI ingredient list is the exception: it stays in INCI.
- **Quebec** has stricter French-language rules, so check them if you sell there.
- Product identity can be left off when it's obvious, like a bar of soap.

## A simple checklist before your first market

1. Finalise the recipe and check it in a lye calculator.
2. Write the ingredient list in INCI names, in order of amount.
3. Make bilingual labels (English and French).
4. File a Cosmetic Notification Form for each product, within 10 days of first sale.
5. Keep a record of the notification date for each recipe.
